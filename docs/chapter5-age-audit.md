# Chapter 5 younger-player text audit

P15 audit: these pieces can exceed two short sentences in the younger mode. Suggestions are for team approval; they have not been substituted for the original longer text. Dynamic values below are examples, and the details they replace should remain available where appropriate. The full scripted journey also records longer paragraphs as a Playwright attachment.

1. **Equator briefing, `power.nightCold`:** “Moon nights near the equator drop to about -173 °C. Brrr! Machines need heaters to survive.” Suggested: “Moon nights are very cold. Machines need heaters.” Keep the temperature available to older players.
2. **Into the dark, turning point:** “Your turning point: Day … Try … In this replay, the crew …” can be three sentences. Suggested: “Try a different choice on Day … See how it changes the mission.”
3. **Storm alert planning:** “Predicted cosmic rays: … An unshielded storm could be anywhere from … A moonwalk can add more. Single-storm line: …” is four sentences. Suggested: “Space radiation changes over time. A moonwalk can add more.” Keep the numerical prediction and limit available to older players.
4. **Storm alert daily results:** a busy day can list several individual alerts, an estimate and the chosen shelter action in more than two sentences. Suggested: “Several space-weather alerts arrived. Check the dose bar and your log.” Preserve each event in the log for inspection.
5. **Communication fact, `communication.radioDelay`:** the greeting “Hello Earth!” plus the delay explanation and comparison can be read as three short sentences. Suggested: “A message to Earth takes a little time. It's like a slow video call.” Keep the delay available to older players.
6. **Can you hear us?, daily results:** “Collected one data point; it is waiting to send. Some particle-event doses are unknown and excluded. Space weather added …” can be three sentences. Suggested: “Your science is waiting to send. Some space-weather details are unknown.” Preserve the measured training dose in the gauge.
7. **Ice to air, daily results:** explanations such as “Melted … The two astronauts used … Some event doses are unknown …” or “The crew rested. The two astronauts used … Space weather added …” can reach three or four sentences. Suggested: “Your choice changed the air supply. Check the bars and log for details.” Preserve the unknown-dose warning when relevant.
