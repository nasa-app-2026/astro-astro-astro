# The game's main chapters

## Title: Building a home on the Moon, step by step

Building a Moon base is like moving to a brand-new place where nobody has ever lived. You don't just turn up with a suitcase. First you look around, then you set up camp, then you learn to survive, then you grow, and finally you learn to look after yourself.

Those are the five stages. Each stage has one or two chapters, which are the missions in the game.

## 🛰️ Stage 1: Exploration — "Let's look before we leap"

You wouldn't jump into a swimming pool without checking how deep it is. Space agencies do the same: they test and look first.

### Chapter 1: Flyby test 🚀

**What happens:** a rocket flies around the Moon and comes back without landing. It's a practice run.

**Real story:**

*   In 2022, NASA's Artemis I flew around the Moon with no people on board. Instead it carried dummies called Helga, Zohar and Moonikin Campos, covered in sensors to measure the radiation a person would get. They measured about 1 mSv a day, which is like about 12 long plane flights every day!
*   In April 2026, Artemis II flew 4 real astronauts around the Moon. When they went behind it, the Moon blocked the radio and they lost contact with Earth for about 40 minutes.
*   Artemis II also tested a laser "space internet" that sent 484 gigabytes of photos, videos and data home.

**Choices in this chapter:** ☀️ radiation (how much dose will the crew get?) and 📡 communication (what happens when Earth can't hear you?).

### Chapter 2: Robots go first 🤖

**What happens:** before people land, robots go to scout. People on Earth set the goals, and the robots carry them out.

**Real story:**

*   In 2009, NASA's LCROSS mission crashed a rocket stage into a dark crater near the south pole on purpose. In the dust it threw up, scientists found water! 💧
*   NASA pays companies to fly small robot landers to the Moon. In March 2025 one of them, IM-2, landed on a mountain called Mons Mouton but tipped over. Landing on the Moon is hard!
*   NASA's water-hunting rover VIPER is planned to go to Mons Mouton in late 2027.
*   Behind hills, robots can't always talk to Earth, so they have to do some things on their own.

**Choices in this chapter:** 📡 communication (the robot must act alone during blackouts) and ⚡ power (the rover's battery).

## ⛺ Stage 2: Establishing a presence — "Let's set up camp"

Now that we know where to go, it's time to send people, and then the stuff they need.

### Chapter 3: First landing 👨‍🚀

**What happens:** astronauts land, explore, do experiments and come home. It's a short visit, like a camping trip.

**Real story:**

*   NASA plans its first south pole landing for 2028. No final landing site has been chosen yet; there are 9 candidate regions.
*   On the Moon, a spacesuit is like a tiny spaceship shaped like a person. It gives air, keeps the right temperature and protects from the Sun.
*   A spacesuit does not stop a big solar storm. If a warning comes, astronauts must get back inside quickly. NASA's plan gives them about 2 hours.

**Choices in this chapter:** 🫁 life support (the suit's air) and ☀️ radiation (when do you head back inside?).

### Chapter 4: Building the outpost ⛺

**What happens:** cargo landers bring supplies, and robots help build, dig and move Moon soil.

**Real story:**

*   Landers are like delivery vans of different sizes:
    *   small robot landers carry about 100–625 kg, like a few big suitcases up to a small car;
    *   bigger landers being built carry about 3 tonnes;
    *   NASA's planned large cargo landers could carry 12–15 tonnes, about the weight of 2 elephants! 🐘🐘
*   A storm shelter that protects 4 astronauts weighs about 1.3–1.6 tonnes. So every shelter takes up space that food or science kit could have used.

**Choices in this chapter:** ⚡ power and ☀️ radiation shielding (is the shelter worth all that weight?).

## 🛡️ Stage 3: Survival — "Can we stay for two weeks?"

### Chapter 5: Survival

**What happens:** the first crew stays for two weeks on a sunny ridge near the south pole, in polar winter.

**Real story:**

*   At the south pole, the Sun doesn't rise and set like at home. It slides around the horizon like a torch circling a room.
*   On the best ridges it shines about 9 days out of 10, but in winter dark spells of a few hours to a few days come every month. 🌑
*   Solar panels only work in sunlight, so the crew must save battery for the dark.
*   Real solar storms from NASA's records can arrive at any time, and the crew must decide when to hide in the shelter.

**Choices in this chapter:** ⚡ power, 🫁 air and ☀️ radiation. Share power between making air, saving for the dark and doing science, and stay safe from storms.

## 🌱 Stage 4: Expansion — "Let's grow!"

### Chapter 6: Expansion 🚙

**What happens:** the outpost gets bigger: more buildings, longer trips and the first gardens.

**Real story:**

*   Japan is designing a pressurised rover, a kind of camper van for the Moon. Astronauts can drive it and live inside without spacesuits.
*   NASA plans a plant experiment called LEAF to see how plants grow on the Moon. 🌱 Scientists on Earth have already grown small plants in real Moon soil from the Apollo missions. They grew, just not as well as in Earth soil.
*   Plants need lots of electricity for grow-lights.
*   When a rover drives far away, hills block the radio. Relay satellites in orbit can pass messages along, like a postman. 📡
*   Later, NASA plans a small nuclear power station of about 40 kW, enough for about 30 homes, that works day and night.

**Choices in this chapter:** 🌱 food (is a garden worth the power?), ⚡ power and 📡 communication (staying in touch on long trips).

## ♻️ Stage 5: Sustainability — "Can we look after ourselves?"

### Chapter 7: Sustainability 💧

**What happens:** the outpost learns to make its own air and water from things found on the Moon, so it doesn't need so many deliveries from Earth.

**Real story:**

*   The dark craters near the south pole hold frozen water ice. One crater floor, Haworth, has probably been dark for over 3 billion years!
*   Ice can be melted into water. Water can be split with electricity into oxygen to breathe and hydrogen.
*   Even Moon rocks and soil are about 40–45% oxygen, locked inside. Scientists are testing ways to get it out.
*   It's like going from buying all your food at the shop to having your own garden and well. 🍎

**Choices in this chapter:** 🫁 life support (making your own oxygen), 🌱 food (growing more) and ⚡ power (all of this needs lots of electricity).
